# Fidelity: art1 items (light, sparkles, particles, fire, film burns)

Filmora items of lanes 1, 3 and 4 that were rendered by a *stand-in* (a
global A/B mix plus a fitted smooth colour field) or not at all, recreated
with our own procedural artwork: light leaks, lens flares, film burns, bokeh,
sparkles, comets and glitter, fire, energy and neon overlays.

## Originality

The artwork is ours. Filmora's installed asset files were never read. The
reference exports were *watched* (contact sheets, coverage and mean colour
over time) to learn each item's concept, timing, motion path, coverage and
palette; shapes are never traced, vectorised or copied from frames. Every
shape, particle system and flare is generated by the WGSL under
`crates/jb-render/src/shaders/art1/` from seeded hashes, noise fields and
simple analytic primitives. No third-party assets are used.

## Machinery

`fx/art1_kit.rs` (shared runtime) and `shaders/art1/kit.wgsl` (noise, glows,
seeded particle systems, comets on Catmull-Rom paths, glitter, bokeh fields,
lens-flare elements, orbs, flame density, simple drawn shapes). Each item is
one WGSL file defining `fx(uv)`, with its keyframes as `//@` comment lines
(see the module docs of `art1_kit.rs`). `//@base ID` composes the artwork over
a registered effect (the fitted colour field of the old stand-in, which keeps
its measured broad tints); `//@pre SIGMA` adds a blurred pre-pass for bloom.
`fx/art1_items.rs` lists the items; `aliases_art1.rs` maps Filmora GUIDs
(exact case) to them. A GUID moves here only when the item looks right on a
side-by-side contact sheet *and* measures no worse than the stand-in; its old
mapping is then removed from the lane alias file, so each GUID has one
mapping.

For development `JB_ART1_WGSL_DIR=crates/jb-render/src/shaders/art1` loads
the WGSL from disk at start-up, so keyframes and shaders can be iterated
without rebuilding.

Particle items (sparkles, glitter, bokeh) stay well above the floor even when
they look right: individual sparks cannot land on the reference's pixels, so
a dense spark field costs MAD wherever it does not coincide. The gain over the
stand-in is mostly in timing and coverage (the comet, band or flash is where
and when the reference has it); the contact sheets are the main check.
